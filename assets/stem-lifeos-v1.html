Personal Adaptive Strategy System

A scientific operating architecture for drastic, compounding life change.

This design externalizes strategic cognition: it converts values and evidence into models, decisions, plans, actions, outcomes, and learning. It is intended to evolve as evidence changes without allowing metrics, automation, or the system itself to become sovereign.

Design correction: Do not become a person who serves a system. Become the sovereign operator of a scientific system that serves your values.

01 / Mission

What the system exists to do

Values → Evidence → Action → Learning

Mission: Build a continuously evolving personal strategy system that turns whole-life evidence into high-quality choices and executable moves while preserving Sai's values, agency, safety, and ability to change direction.

VALUES → EVIDENCE → MODELS → DECISIONS → PLANS → ACTIONS → OUTCOMES → LEARNING
Plan like chess

Sequence and dependencies

Model prerequisites, countermoves, resource conflicts, downstream consequences, and conditional branches.

Reason like poker

Uncertainty and hidden information

Use base rates, probabilities when defensible, asymmetric bets, option value, and explicit unknowns.

Learn like science

Prediction and correction

Form falsifiable hypotheses, run bounded experiments, reconcile outcomes, and retire failed models.

Objective portfolio — not one life score

The system should improve capability, health and resilience, agency, knowledge and judgment, financial and career optionality, relationships and social capital, meaningful impact, and compounding rate. These remain separate objectives on a Pareto frontier so one convenient metric cannot consume the whole life.

02 / Constitution

The rules above optimization

Highest authority layer

Before the system can optimize anything, it must know what it is forbidden to sacrifice. The constitution will eventually hold Sai's values, anti-goals, non-negotiables, risk limits, and definitions of success.

  1. Sai retains sovereignty. The system recommends; Sai authorizes.
  2. Values outrank metrics. Measurements describe reality; they do not define what deserves a life.
  3. Evidence outranks narrative. Claims remain distinguishable from calculations, inferences, and unknowns.
  4. Decision quality differs from outcome quality. Luck must not teach the wrong lesson.
  5. Prefer reversible experiments. Buy information before making avoidable irreversible commitments.
  6. Attack the active constraint. Improving a non-constraint creates motion without system-level progress.
  7. Expose trade-offs. Every major recommendation names its nearest alternative and opportunity cost.
  8. No model is sacred. Confidence rises or falls through predictions, evidence, and outcomes.
  9. No hidden autonomy. The system cannot silently execute, change authority, or rewrite its constitution.
  10. Protect hard constraints. Health, legality, financial survival, relationships, and recovery capacity override speed.
03 / Architecture

Closed-loop personal strategy system

Auditable / adaptive / human-governed

The architecture separates evidence, interpretation, choice, execution, and learning. That separation prevents attractive stories from becoming facts and prevents individual outcomes from rewriting prior reasoning.

Evidence classes
  • Observed: directly recorded fact
  • Calculated: reproducible transformation
  • Inferred: interpretation with assumptions
  • External: research, expert evidence, base rates
  • Unknown: unavailable or unresolved information
Dynamic personal state
  • Goals, values, commitments, opportunities
  • Capabilities, skills, resources, and demonstrated strengths
  • Time, energy, attention, money, and social support
  • Habits, schedule, environment, constraints, and dependencies
  • Past decisions, active hypotheses, uncertainty, and outcomes

Life domains should be derived dynamically from evidence. They may appear, merge, split, become dormant, or become strategically dominant; a fixed task taxonomy must not define the whole person.

04 / Modules

Seven components of V1

Each has one irreducible job
A

Evidence Ledger

What is actually known?

Stores atomic observations with source, timestamp, freshness, evidence class, quality, and contradictions. Interpretations never masquerade as facts.

B

Dynamic State Model

What position is Sai currently in?

Maintains a bounded personal “digital twin” of resources, constraints, commitments, capabilities, opportunities, relationships, and uncertainty—without pretending to perfectly simulate a life.

C

Scientific Reasoning Engine

Which mechanisms best explain the position?

Routes serious observations through multiple relevant expert families, preserves disagreement, and rejects contributions that cannot trace to evidence or change a real choice.

D

Strategy and Move-Tree Engine

What moves could change the state?

Generates alternatives, includes no-action, maps dependencies and responses, compares optionality and downside, and keeps later branches conditional.

E

Decision Compiler

Which move deserves commitment?

Classifies decisions by impact and reversibility, exposes trade-offs, runs premortems, and defines review or reversal conditions before commitment.

F

Plan and Action Compiler

What exact move happens next?

Translates a decision into dependencies, a first physical or digital step, a bounded action, an observable done condition, and a fallback.

G

Outcome-Learning Engine

What did reality teach the system?

Separates process quality from outcome quality, reconciles predictions, estimates the contribution of skill versus randomness, and strengthens, weakens, contradicts, generalizes, or retires hypotheses without deleting history.

05 / Decision Protocol

Different decisions receive different rigor

No bureaucracy for trivial choices
Decision classDefault treatmentRequired protection
Low-impact + reversibleDefault rule or rapid checklistTimebox; do not over-analyze.
High-impact + reversibleBounded experimentSuccess, failure, cost, and review signals.
Low-impact + irreversibleDeliberate reviewConfirm necessity and nearest reversible substitute.
High-impact + irreversibleFull decision packetPremortem, cooling period, outside expertise when appropriate, explicit approval.

Define

State the real decision, desired outcome, protected values, constraints, and decision deadline.

Establish Evidence

Separate observations, calculations, inferences, external base rates, and unknowns.

Generate Options

Create genuine alternatives—including doing nothing or buying more information.

Model Consequences

Compare upside, downside, optionality, dependencies, second-order effects, and response branches.

Challenge

Run a premortem, seek disconfirming evidence, test reverse causality, and surface model disagreement.

Select

Choose the strongest risk-adjusted move and state why it dominates the nearest alternative.

Pre-register

Record confidence only when defensible, plus success, failure, pivot, stop, and reversal conditions.

Compile

Produce the first action, dependencies, bounded effort, observable completion, and fallback.

Reconcile

Judge the process with information available at the time; then update the model from outcomes.

06 / Operating Loop

Rolling-horizon control, not rigid life planning

Precise near-term / conditional long-term

The system should use model-predictive control: make the immediate moves precise, leave distant moves conditional, observe the new state, and replan whenever meaningful evidence changes.

07 / V1 Outputs

One compact Life Strategy Board

Whole-life synthesis before tasks

The visible output should explain Sai's position, real choices, protected values, and concrete moves. Tasks remain downstream implementation—not the center of the synthesis.

Your Position

A concise whole-life synthesis of trajectory, tension, leverage, evidence quality, and uncertainty.

Current Constraint

The bottleneck whose improvement would most change overall system performance.

Winning Moves

Up to three ranked choices with why now, expected unlock, trade-off, evidence, models, win condition, and reversal condition.

Hidden Leverage

Non-obvious cross-domain mechanisms and opportunities that isolated task review would miss.

Three Horizons

Detailed immediate moves, the current operating cycle, and a conditional strategic horizon.

Pivot Signals

Facts that reverse a recommendation, change priority, or unlock the next move.

Unknowns

Information gaps where acquiring evidence may have higher expected value than acting.

Downstream Actions

Only after strategy: first step, dependencies, bounded effort, done condition, expected state change, and fallback.

08 / Scientific Quality Gates

What “Ivy League grade” means here

Methodological rigor, not ornate language
Terms and objectives are defined before reasoning begins.
Assumptions and dependencies are explicit.
Every consequential claim has source provenance.
Base rates and comparison classes are considered.
Alternative explanations and reverse causality are tested.
Disconfirming evidence is actively sought.
Correlation is not silently upgraded to causation.
Premortems expose failure paths before commitment.
Every hypothesis has an observable falsifier when feasible.
Opportunity cost and the nearest alternative are visible.
Stop, review, pivot, or reversal conditions are predeclared.
Due predictions and outcomes are reconciled without rewriting history.
Valid system conclusion

“The available evidence does not justify a decision yet. The highest-value move is to acquire specific information.” The system must never manufacture certainty merely to produce an action.

09 / Boundaries

V1 scope and non-goals

YAGNI / safety / anti-Goodhart

V1 is designed to

  • Map the whole-life position from attributable evidence.
  • Identify the current constraint and high-leverage options.
  • Produce explicit decisions, trade-offs, and conditional plans.
  • Compile selected choices into bounded observable moves.
  • Learn from forecasts, experiments, actions, and outcomes.
  • Preserve uncertainty and human approval boundaries.

V1 must not

  • Autonomously make or execute irreversible decisions.
  • Diagnose psychological or medical conditions.
  • Optimize every hour or reduce life to one score.
  • Generate artificial probabilities or causal certainty.
  • Reward activity instead of real state change.
  • Change its constitution, authority, data access, or risk limits without approval.
